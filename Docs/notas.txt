  ✅ Most Relevant Variables for Respiratory Illness Prediction
1️⃣ Key Symptoms (Direct Indicators of Respiratory Illness)

    Fiebre (Fever) → Common in infections, including respiratory illnesses.

    Disnea (Shortness of breath) → Strong indicator of respiratory distress.

    Tos (Cough) → Key symptom of most respiratory infections. (Wasn't listed, but highly relevant!)

    Rinorrea (Runny nose) → Suggests upper respiratory tract involvement.

    Polipnea (Rapid breathing) → Indicates severe respiratory involvement.

    Cianosis (Bluish skin due to lack of oxygen) → Severe respiratory failure indicator.

2️⃣ Supporting Symptoms (Possible Indicators)

    Dolor torácico (Chest pain) → May indicate severe respiratory infection.

    Escalofríos (Chills) → Suggests systemic infection.

    Cefalea (Headache) → Sometimes linked to viral respiratory infections.

    Mialgias / Artralgias (Muscle/joint pain) → Common in flu-like illnesses.

    Ataque al estado general (General discomfort) → Non-specific but relevant.

    Inicio súbito (Sudden onset) → Suggests acute infections.

3️⃣ Risk Factors (Increase the likelihood of Severe Disease)

    EPOC (Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease) → High-risk for complications.

    Asma (Asthma) → Increased risk of respiratory complications.

    Inmunosupresión (Immunosuppression) → Higher risk of severe infections.

    Hipertensión (Hypertension) → Associated with worse COVID-19 outcomes.

    Diabetes → Increases susceptibility to infections.

    Enfermedad cardiaca (Heart disease) → Affects oxygenation and respiratory function.

    Obesidad (Obesity) → A known risk factor for respiratory complications.

    Insuficiencia renal crónica (Chronic kidney disease) → Affects immune response.

    Tabaquismo (Smoking) → Strongly linked to lung disease and infections.

❌ Less Relevant Variables

These may not be directly useful for prediction:

    Odinofagia (Sore throat) → More common in non-respiratory infections.

    Irritabilidad (Irritability) → Non-specific.

    Diarrea / Vómito / Dolor abdominal → More associated with gastrointestinal infections.

    Conjuntivitis (Eye inflammation) → Can be viral but not a key predictor.

    Anosmia / Disgeusia (Loss of smell/taste) → COVID-19-specific, but not general.

    VIH/SIDA (HIV/AIDS) → Related to immunosuppression but less direct.

    Otra condición (Other conditions) → Too vague to be predictive.

Why Does SMOTE Apply in Each Fold?

    workflow_map("fit_resamples", resamples = cv_folds, ...) ensures that the recipe (recipe_prep) is applied separately for each fold.

    step_smote(target, over_ratio = 1) is part of the recipe, so SMOTE is re-applied for each fold before training the model.

    This is correct behavior because SMOTE should be applied only on the training data of each fold (not on the whole dataset before splitting).
